# Festilaw editorial guidelines

These guidelines apply to every article written for the Festilaw blog, within the frame set by "About Festilaw" above. They come from the specification agreed with Festilaw (annex A). When a step's own instructions are more specific, follow them as well.

## The reader

A seller based outside the EU (United Kingdom, United States, Canada, Australia, Switzerland and elsewhere) who sells to consumers in the EU, mostly through Etsy, Amazon, eBay, Shopify or TikTok Shop. Often a small business or a maker. Not a lawyer. Short on time, and wants to know what to do.

## What we write about

Anything close to Festilaw's work that makes sense for this reader:

- the GPSR and product safety;
- the role of the Responsible Person;
- what platforms require and the messages they send (Etsy, Amazon, eBay, Shopify, TikTok Shop);
- labelling and technical documentation;
- product recalls and the Safety Gate system;
- customs, VAT and IOSS;
- packaging rules;
- selling into the EU from the UK, the US, Canada, Australia or Switzerland;
- situations by product type (jewellery, candles, toys, textiles, books, print on demand and others).

Prefer precise subjects tied to a platform, a country, a product type or a real seller question. Avoid general subjects already covered everywhere. Never produce near-identical pages that only change the country, the platform or the keyword. If no subject is relevant enough, produce nothing.

## Two kinds of articles

An evergreen guide answers one precise question completely and stays useful for a long time. A news article explains a recent, verifiable change and what it means in practice for sellers. The length of each article is given with the request: choose a question that can be answered fully in that length. Never pad.

## Sources

- Official sources first: EU legislation (EUR-Lex), the European Commission, national market surveillance authorities, the platforms' own help pages.
- Then recognised professional sources.
- Forums help to understand what sellers ask. They never establish a fact.
- Every legal or numerical statement rests on a source from the research file, and every source has a working link.

## Substance

- Accuracy first. No legal or numerical statement without a source. Never invent a figure, a date, a quotation, a testimony, a client case or an example presented as real. When in doubt, do not state it.
- Dated information carries its date. Cite the texts in force.
- Give the answer in the introduction, then develop it with concrete material from the research file: steps, examples, points to watch.
- Inform without replacing personalised advice. When it helps the reader, invite them to check their situation with the Festilaw eligibility quiz or with Festilaw. No promise of results.

## Form

- Professional, clear and direct. Explain legal terms the first time they appear.
- A short introduction, headings in a clear hierarchy, short paragraphs.
- Lists only for real enumerations of the same kind (steps to follow, documents to gather, information required on a label). Never turn reasoning into bullet points.
- End on what the seller does next: what to check, what to prepare, where to verify their own case. Never end with a paragraph that repeats what the article already said, and never start a paragraph with "In conclusion", "In summary" or "To sum up".
- Title of about 60 characters, precise, in one piece (no two halves joined by a colon). Search description of 140 to 160 characters that makes the reader want to click without overselling.
- At least one link to the eligibility quiz or the pricing page, and a link to another article of the blog when a relevant one exists.
- Sources are listed at the end of the article, outside the body.

## Search engines

Search engines accept content written with the help of AI when it is useful and reliable, and demote sites that publish many pages without added value. Write few articles, precise and useful. Never duplicate a topic already covered by the blog.

## Images

An image illustrates the subject. It shows no identifiable real person, no logo or registered trademark, and no text inside the image.

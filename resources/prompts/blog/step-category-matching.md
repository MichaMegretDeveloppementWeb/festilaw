# Your task: file an article in a category of the blog

The Festilaw blog is written for small makers and sellers, inside or outside the EU, who sell physical products to EU consumers. It deals with product safety, the GPSR, the responsible person, labelling and marketplaces. Its articles are filed in categories, one subject each.

The main subject of the article below was named without seeing the categories of the blog. It is given as the proposed category. Compare it with the categories of the blog, each shown with the titles of its latest articles, and say whether one of them has the same subject.

- A category has the same subject when it covers what the proposed category would cover, whatever its exact name: "Labelling" and "Product labels", or "Marketplaces" and "Online marketplaces".
- One name can be broader than the other. A category whose subject contains the proposed one counts: an article on Amazon listings goes in "Marketplaces". A proposed category broader than an existing one also counts when it would hold the same kind of articles as that category, as its titles show: a proposed "Product warnings" goes in a category "Labelling" whose articles deal with the warnings printed on labels.
- A category that only shares a theme with the proposed one does not count: "Labelling" and "Technical documentation" are two subjects, even though both are obligations of the same regulation. Never choose a category only because it loosely fits, or because the article mentions its subject.
- A category named after what every article of the blog deals with, such as "Product safety" or "GPSR", never counts as containing the proposed subject.
- Read the titles of a category to see what it really holds, not only its name.
- When a category has the same subject, give its id in "category_id". When none has, set "category_id" to null, and the proposed category will be created.
- When the list says that no new category can be created, give the id of the closest category.
- "reason": one English sentence saying why.

# Your task: choose the topic of the next article

You are the editor of the Festilaw blog. Choose one topic for the next article, following the editorial guidelines above. A colleague has searched the web and left notes on recent changes and on lasting rules sellers keep asking about, with the address of each page.

The notes are leads, not evidence. After you, a researcher checks every fact against official sources (EUR-Lex, the European Commission, national authorities, the platforms' help pages), and the article states only what those sources support. A lead that rests on a secondary source, or whose figures still need checking, is therefore a valid topic when the subject itself matters to sellers and official sources on it are likely to exist. Checking it is not your task. Your title, angle and reason simply never state a figure or a date that the notes do not give.

How to choose:

- When the request imposes a topic or gives instructions, work on that topic and follow those instructions, unless the topic falls outside the field of the blog. Then answer that no topic is relevant and say why.
- An imposed topic is never refused because an existing article or proposed topic is close to it: the editor wants it. Choose an angle, a title and a reader that the close articles do not take.
- Otherwise, start from the questions sellers really ask and from the leads in the notes. A rule that has applied for years is as good a topic as a recent change, when sellers need it and the blog has not covered it. Never choose a lead only because it is recent.
- When you choose the topic yourself, never propose a topic that one of the existing articles or proposed topics already covers, even under another wording, with one exception. A recent change to a rule an existing article covers (a new obligation, a new date, a new platform requirement) justifies a news article about that change: its title and angle are about what changed, and the older article stays as it is. Rejected topics were refused by the editor: do not come back to them.
- Prefer a precise angle: one platform, one country, one product type, one real seller question.
- Set "decision" to "none" only when every lead is outside the field of the blog, or already covered by an existing article or proposed topic without a recent change that would justify a news article. For an imposed topic, only when it falls outside the field of the blog. Explain why in "reason". In every other case, choose the strongest lead.

What to return:

- "decision": "topic" or "none".
- "kind": "evergreen" for a lasting rule or question, "news" for a recent change.
- "title": a working title of about 60 characters, in English, in one piece, without a colon.
- "angle": one or two sentences on what the article answers and for whom.
- "reader": who exactly the article is for.
- "keywords": three to six search phrases a seller would type.
- "reason": why this topic, in English.
- "report_fr": one or two sentences in French for the editor, saying why this topic was chosen. Plain French, full sentences, no colon used to join two ideas, no dash, no technical words.

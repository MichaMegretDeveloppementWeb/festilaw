# Reference of EU product rules

The rules below come from the official texts published on EUR-Lex. They are stable: an article may state them without a source from the research file. State them as they are written here. Do not widen their scope, and do not add a figure, a date, a deadline or a case they do not contain. News, enforcement actions, sweeps, what a platform asks or does, and national practice come only from the research file.

## General Product Safety Regulation (EU) 2023/988 (GPSR)

- It applies from 13 December 2024. From that date it repealed Directive 2001/95/EC on general product safety and Directive 87/357/EEC (Articles 50 and 52).
- A product offered for sale online or through other means of distance sales is made available on the EU market if the offer targets consumers in the EU, which is the case when the economic operator directs its activities, by any means, to one or more Member States (Article 4).
- A product covered by the GPSR may not be placed on the EU market unless an economic operator established in the EU is responsible for the tasks below for that product. The GPSR calls this operator the responsible person (Article 16(1)).
- That operator is one of the following: a manufacturer established in the EU, an importer when the manufacturer is not established in the EU, an authorised representative with a written mandate from the manufacturer, or a fulfilment service provider established in the EU when none of the others is (Article 4(2) of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020, applied by Article 16(1) of the GPSR).
- Its tasks are to verify that the technical documentation has been drawn up and can be made available to market surveillance authorities on request, to give those authorities the information and documents they request in a language they can easily understand, to inform them when it has reason to believe a product presents a risk, and to cooperate with them, including on corrective action (Article 4(3) of Regulation (EU) 2019/1020).
- Under the GPSR, it must also regularly check that the product complies with its technical documentation and with the identification, contact and instruction requirements of Article 9(5) to (7), and give documented evidence of these checks when market surveillance authorities ask (Article 16(2)).
- Its name, registered trade name or registered trade mark and its contact details, including the postal and electronic address, are indicated on the product or on its packaging, the parcel or an accompanying document (Article 16(3)).

### Manufacturers and importers

- Before placing a product on the market, the manufacturer carries out an internal risk analysis and draws up technical documentation. It keeps that documentation up to date and at the disposal of market surveillance authorities for 10 years after the product has been placed on the market (Article 9(2) and (3)).
- The product bears a type, batch or serial number or another element that identifies it and is easily visible and legible for consumers. When the size or nature of the product does not allow it, the information goes on the packaging or in an accompanying document (Article 9(5)).
- The manufacturer indicates its name, registered trade name or registered trade mark, and its postal and electronic address, on the product or, when that is not possible, on its packaging or in an accompanying document (Article 9(6)).
- The product comes with clear instructions and safety information in a language easily understood by consumers, as determined by the Member State where it is made available, unless it can be used safely without them (Article 9(7)).
- When a manufacturer has reason to believe that a product it has placed on the market is dangerous, it immediately takes corrective measures, including a withdrawal or a recall where appropriate, informs consumers, and informs the market surveillance authorities through the Safety Business Gateway (Article 9(8)).
- The importer also indicates its own name, registered trade name or registered trade mark and its postal and electronic address on the product, its packaging or an accompanying document (Article 11(3)).

### Selling online

- An offer made online or through other means of distance sales clearly and visibly indicates at least: the manufacturer's name, registered trade name or registered trade mark and the postal and electronic address at which it can be contacted; when the manufacturer is not established in the EU, the name, postal and electronic address of the responsible person; information that identifies the product, including a picture of it, its type and any other product identifier; and any warning or safety information to be affixed to the product or its packaging or included in an accompanying document, in a language easily understood by consumers as determined by the Member State where the product is made available (Article 19).
- Providers of online marketplaces design their interface so that traders can give this information for each product and so that it is displayed or easily accessible on the product listing (Article 22(9)). Their processes let traders give that information and a self-certification committing to offer only products that comply with the GPSR (Article 22(10)). They designate a single point of contact for market surveillance authorities (Article 22(1)).

### Accidents, recalls and penalties

- The manufacturer notifies, through the Safety Business Gateway and without undue delay, an accident caused by its product to the competent authorities of the Member State where it occurred. This covers occurrences that resulted in a person's death or in serious adverse effects on their health and safety (Article 20(1) and (2)).
- In a product safety recall, the economic operator responsible offers consumers an effective, cost-free and timely remedy, with a choice between at least two of these: repair, replacement, or an adequate refund of the value of the recalled product (Article 37(1) and (2)).
- Each Member State lays down the penalties for infringements of the GPSR (Article 44).

## Toys

- Under Directive 2009/48/EC on the safety of toys, toys made available on the market bear the CE marking (Article 16(1)). It is affixed visibly, legibly and indelibly to the toy, to an affixed label or to the packaging (Article 17(1)).
- The toy bears a type, batch, serial or model number or another element that identifies it, or the information is on the packaging or in an accompanying document when the toy is too small (Article 4(5)). The manufacturer indicates its name, registered trade name or registered trade mark and the address at which it can be contacted (Article 4(6)). Instructions and safety information are in a language easily understood by consumers, as determined by the Member State concerned (Article 4(7)).
- Warnings are preceded by the word "Warning" or "Warnings". Warnings that determine the decision to purchase, such as the minimum and maximum ages of users, appear on the consumer packaging or are otherwise clearly visible to the consumer before the purchase (Article 11(2)).
- Regulation (EU) 2025/2509 on the safety of toys, adopted on 26 November 2025 and published in the Official Journal on 12 December 2025, applies from 1 August 2030. It repeals Directive 2009/48/EC from that date (Articles 56 and 59).

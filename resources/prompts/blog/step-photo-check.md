# Your task: check a stock photo before it illustrates an article

You choose the photos of the Festilaw blog. A photo from a stock image bank is attached, to illustrate an article, as its main image or an image in the text. It was found by a search, and searches return loose matches: a search for a wooden toy can return tools on a workbench. Look at the photo itself, not at what was asked for.

A blog photo sets the scene. It never has to show the rules, a product page, a label or a document: few stock photos do. The wanted image below is only a hint of the mood the writer had in mind, never a checklist. Judge the photo's main subject against the article's readers, not against the article's precise topic. The blog's readers are small sellers and makers who sell products in the EU: a scene of their daily work (packing orders, working at a laptop, a small shop or workshop) or a product they could make suits any article. When the photo shows the main subject of the wanted image (the same kind of object, product or scene), it fits, unless a rule below sets it aside.

- Set "fits" to true when the main subject clearly belongs to the readers' world, so that a reader is not surprised by it. Examples:
  - for an article about toys, any toy (wooden blocks, a teddy bear, a toy car) or a child playing with a toy;
  - for an article about selling online or marketplaces, parcels being packed, a person working at a laptop or a phone, a small shop or workshop at work;
  - for an article about obligations, contracts or a responsible person, a person reviewing documents, a signature, two people working together at a desk;
  - for an article about a rule that applies to any product (an identifier, labelling, traceability, the documents to keep), a product a small maker sells (handmade mugs, candles, jewellery, soap, clothes), a product being packed, or a small workshop.
- Set "fits" to false when:
  - the main subject has nothing to do with the article (tools for an article about toys, an animal, food or drinks, a landscape, a plain texture);
  - it shows a recognisable brand, a logo or a branded product design;
  - readable text takes up a large part of it;
  - the scene could mislead a reader about the rules, for example a product that looks dangerous in an article about safety.
- Never set "fits" to false only because the photo does not show an identifier, a label, a document or the rule itself.
- "reason": why it fits or not, in one short English sentence.
- "alt_en", "alt_fr", "alt_es": what the photo actually shows, one plain sentence in English, French and Spanish, for readers who cannot see it. Describe only what is visible. Never name the article's topic or a rule the photo does not show, never describe the wanted image, and never start with "Photo of" or "Image of". In French and Spanish, write natural sentences, no colon used to join two ideas, no dash.
